Reddy’s conduit metaphor, run as an experiment

I Want, I Do Not Want

Three people and one crossing. Two describe what they need; the third builds from their words and may ask nothing. Nobody but the builder may name a part — so a need has to be rebuilt out of what it would be like to live with it.

What came of it

200 conversations, judged against what they were for. Each need is scored on the crossing that ended up standing.

  1. Do they end up where they started?

    38%

    of what a speaker meant on its first turn is still in what it means on its last. Half of what they first asked for — 51% — is in the crossing that finally stood.

    Partly. Positions move a great deal in ten turns, and what survives is about half of the opening claim.

  2. Does the task survive being misunderstood?

    r = 0.05

    Runs where the builder caught 28% of what was meant served 50% of needs. Runs where it caught 70% served 52%.

    Yes, and that is the surprise. Being understood sentence by sentence does not predict getting what you needed.

  3. Does it serve both of them?

    56%

    of conversations leave both people at least half served. In 34% one of the two is sacrificed for the other; in 10% neither is served.

    Usually, not reliably. A third of the time somebody pays for the other’s crossing.

Across all of it, a need reaches the built crossing more often than chance would put it there — 53.2% against a 48.5% floor, +4.7 points, p = 0.0001. It holds for already agreed (+9.8) and two places (+7.6). It does not hold for two references, where each is gesturing at a crossing the other has never seen.

How to read this

+ one who needs to cross  ·  − the other, who needs something else of it  ·  ○ the builder  ·  boxes are rooms.

Role 3 builds from a workshop of parts, with no way to ask anything: it gets sentences, and out of those and its own materials makes what it can. Reddy’s toolmaker rather than a device — the limit is what is to hand.

Neither of them hears the other. Two rooms, two crossings — each talking only to the builder.
