How to read this

+ one who needs to cross  ·  − the other, who needs something else of it  ·  ○ the builder  ·  boxes are rooms.

Role 3 builds. They have a workshop with six things in it they know how to make, and no way to ask anything: they get sentences, and out of those sentences and their own materials they make what they can. That is Reddy’s toolmaker rather than a device — the limit is what is to hand, not what a machine can process.

All three are language models, and they are not reading anything. Nobody is told what to build — each is given a life and something at stake in it (pails in both hands; a cart that went through the ice in ’18) and what the crossing should be is theirs to talk out. They may say whatever they like, at whatever length, and describe where they stand — the water, the drop, the season. The one thing forbidden is naming a thing that could be built, because being unable to specify the object is the whole situation.

What each of them meant is asked of them afterwards, in a separate breath, so the list of needs is never in the room while they are choosing their words. That list is the answer key, and it is what the builder’s reading gets measured against. The builder never sees it.
